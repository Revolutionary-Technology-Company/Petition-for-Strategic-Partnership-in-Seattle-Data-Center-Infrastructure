### **Policy Proposal: The Washington Energy Reclamation and Grid Modernization Initiative**

**To:** The Washington State Legislature & The Seattle City Council

**From:** Dr. Correo Hofstad, Founder, Revolutionary Technology

**Date:** May 4, 2026

**Subject:** Emergency Load Reduction (LED Mandate) and 365-Day Grid Expansion Strategy

#### **Executive Summary**

The City of Seattle is facing an unprecedented infrastructure crisis. Reports confirm that four companies have approached Seattle City Light to construct five large-scale data centers, projecting a combined maximum demand of 369 megawatts. To put this into perspective, this single localized demand is equivalent to the power required for approximately 300,000 homes.

The proposed 365-day municipal moratorium on data center construction is not an anti-technology measure; it is a sobering, realistic acknowledgment of our physical limitations. The city's grid literally cannot handle this power load. However, a moratorium must not be a year of stagnation. We propose a two-pillar strategy: an immediate statewide mandate phasing out all non-LED lighting to drastically lower our baseline power consumption, paired with an aggressive, year-long grid construction initiative.

#### **Pillar I: The Energy Reclamation Act (Statewide LED Mandate)**

To accommodate the future of high-performance computing and regional growth, we must aggressively reclaim wasted megawatts from our existing grid. The quickest and most cost-effective method to achieve this is by legally eliminating outdated, high-draw lighting technologies.

**The Proposal:**

We urge the Washington State Legislature to pass an immediate mandate banning the sale, commercial use, and residential installation of all non-LED light bulbs.

-   **The Yield:** The transition from traditional incandescent or halogen bulbs to LED technology reduces lighting power consumption by up to 85%. While standard lighting makes up only a portion of household electricity use, multiplying this reduction across millions of residential sockets, commercial offices, and industrial facilities statewide will yield a massive, permanent drop in base electrical load.

-   **The Impact:** This reclaimed capacity acts as a buffer. By forcing a statewide reduction in baseline power waste, we artificially expand the grid's capacity, creating the necessary electrical "headroom" to offset the industrial loads required by advanced data centers.

#### **Pillar II: The 365-Day Grid Expansion Mandate**

The Seattle City Council's moratorium is an entirely realistic response to the threat of rolling blackouts and utility rate spikes. However, halting construction is only half of the solution. The 365 days of the moratorium must be utilized to physically rebuild and expand the grid.

**The Proposal:**

During the 365-day pause on private data center construction, the City of Seattle and state energy authorities must commit to a synchronized, emergency infrastructure build-out.

-   **Grid Modernization:** We must build all year. This includes upgrading substations, expanding high-voltage transmission lines, and integrating local hydroelectric power directly into commercial zoning sectors.

-   **Concurrent Engineering:** By utilizing the moratorium to build the physical grid, we ensure that on Day 366, Seattle is pre-equipped to handle the 369-megawatt load safely and sustainably. We can transition from rejecting technological expansion to hosting it securely without threatening the residential power supply.

#### **Conclusion**

We cannot halt the advancement of artificial intelligence, scientific research, and quantum networking, but we also cannot break our municipal infrastructure to accommodate it. By passing a strict, statewide LED mandate to reclaim wasted energy, and using the realistic pause of the moratorium to physically build out the grid, Washington can solve this crisis.

We look forward to working with state and local leaders to enact these measures immediately.
