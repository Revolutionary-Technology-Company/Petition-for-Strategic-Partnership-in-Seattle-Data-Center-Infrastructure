This list includes abandoned, non-operational, or "deadbeat" hydroelectric opportunities in Washington state that remain in place as of early 2026, offering potential for rehabilitation or re-powering, excluding sites that have already been removed. \[[1](https://www.kuow.org/stories/washington-once-saw-hydropower-as-an-easy-alternative-to-coal-that-s-changing), [2](https://www.edf.org/media/trump-administration-forces-washingtons-last-coal-plant-stay-open-longer-data-proves-its-not#:~:text=In%20Washington%20state%2C%20hydropower%20facilities%20produced%20more,plant%20\)%20plant%20%E2%80%93%20produced%200%20GWh.)\]

**Top Abandoned/Non-Operational Hydroelectric Opportunities**

* **Enloe Dam (Similkameen River):** A 1920s-era concrete dam near Oroville, WA, that has not generated power for decades but remains in place.  
* **Electron Dam (Puyallup River):** Built in 1904, this facility has faced operational challenges, ownership changes, and environmental litigation, leaving its long-term future as a generating facility uncertain despite the structure remaining in place.  
* **Newhalem Creek Hydro Project (Skagit River watershed):** While partial removal of infrastructure is planned, the historic powerhouse and penstock may be retained. The project has not consistently produced power since 2010 due to landslides and damage.  
* **Tumwater Falls Dam (Deschutes River):** Located in Tumwater, this 1900s dam is no longer used for power generation but remains a significant site within a state park.  
* **Mill Pond Dam:** Though partially removed, the site in the Sullivan Creek drainage previously hosted a 1909 hydropower plant and represents a rehabilitated stream/potential small hydro site. \[[3](https://www.underscore.news/land/the-pacific-northwest-is-littered-with-deadbeat-dams/), [4](https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/climate-lab/why-deadbeat-dam-removals-are-so-difficult-in-wa-pnw/), [5](https://apps.ecology.wa.gov/publications/documents/94016.pdf), [6](https://www.seattle.gov/city-light/energy/power-supply-and-delivery/hydroelectric-resources/newhalem-creek-hydroelectric-project), [7](https://www.underscore.news/land/the-pacific-northwest-is-littered-with-deadbeat-dams/#:~:text=Enloe%20Dam%20is%20photographed%20near%20the%20town%20of%20Oroville%2C%20Wash.%20Wednesday%2C%20May%2022%2C%202024.)\]

Potential Non-Powered Dam (NPD) Retrofits \[[8](https://www.washingtonpost.com/climate-solutions/2022/05/06/dams-nonpowered-hydropower-energy/#:~:text=This%20is%20good%20news%20for%20existing%20hydropower,need%20other%20than%20hydropower%2C%20for%20electricity%20production.)\]

**Washington has over 1,345 dams, and many non-powered dams (NPDs) are considered prime for retrofitting.**

* **Skookumchuck Dam (Skookumchuck River):** Built in 1970 for a coal power plant, it is still in place and has potential for power generation.  
* **Lochsloy Dam (Lake Lochsloy):** Constructed in 1955, currently used for agricultural purposes, and remains in place.  
* **Various Irrigation District Dams:** Several agricultural diversion dams in the Yakima and Wenatchee valleys are candidates for small-scale hydro modernization. \[[4](https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/climate-lab/why-deadbeat-dam-removals-are-so-difficult-in-wa-pnw/)\]

**Other Sites and Context**

* **Georgetown Steam Plant:** While not a dam, this 1906 industrial site in Seattle is a preserved, abandoned facility formerly linked to the city's early hydroelectric grid.  
* **Removed/Not Included:** Sites removed include the Elwha Dam, Glines Canyon Dam, Pilchuck Dam, Middle Fork Nooksack, and Kwoneesum Dam. \[[4](https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/climate-lab/why-deadbeat-dam-removals-are-so-difficult-in-wa-pnw/), [11](https://www.abandonedwashingtonstate.com/industry.html), [12](https://www.nps.gov/olym/learn/nature/elwha-ecosystem-restoration.htm)\]
