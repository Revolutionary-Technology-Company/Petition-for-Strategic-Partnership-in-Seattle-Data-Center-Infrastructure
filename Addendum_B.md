### **Addendum B: Infrastructure Preservation and Investor Accountability Framework**

**To:** The Seattle City Council & Department of Construction and Inspections (SDCI)
**Subject:** Mitigation of Municipal Liability and Physical Asset Degradation During Proposed Moratorium

#### **1. The "Weather-Tight" Construction Exemption**
A blunt, immediate cessation of all data center construction in the Pacific Northwest is physically destructive and fiscally irresponsible. Seattle’s climate dictates that exposing partially completed commercial structures to prolonged rainy seasons results in irreversible water intrusion, mold propagation, and structural degradation. 

**Policy Demand:** The proposed moratorium must include an immediate **Infrastructure Preservation Exemption**. Any data center project currently under construction or in the advanced permitting stages must be legally permitted to continue physical construction until the building envelope is fully sealed and weather-tight. 
* We must finish and seal the buildings. Halting the installation of servers and grid connections to protect the 369-megawatt threshold is a separate regulatory action from halting concrete, steel, and roofing work. The City can restrict the power draw without artificially rotting the physical capital already invested in the ground.

#### **2. Municipal Liability and Investor Compensation**
The current grid crisis is a result of municipal under-calculation, not private sector malfeasance. Institutional investors and technology partners committed capital to the Seattle area based on the City's own published utility capabilities and initial zoning approvals.

If Seattle City Light and municipal planners failed to accurately forecast the power grid's capacity to support the digital economy, the financial penalty for that failure cannot be placed entirely on the investors who acted in good faith. 

**Policy Demand:**
If the City Council enforces a hard stop on construction that prevents buildings from reaching a sealed, weather-proof state, or revokes permits previously green-lit under earlier utility assessments, the City must establish a **Capital Compensation Fund**. 
* The City must be prepared to compensate investors for the accelerated depreciation of exposed assets, sunk labor costs, and capital flight caused by sudden regulatory whiplash. 
* By allowing physical construction to continue to completion—while pausing only the final electrical integration until the grid is modernized or our off-grid hydroelectric solutions (Andro Hydro) are connected—the City avoids massive class-action liabilities and maintains its reputation as a reliable partner for global technology investment.

#### **3. Conclusion: A Phased Reality**
We can protect the power grid without punishing the builders. Let the physical construction finish so the assets are protected from the Washington weather. Keep the investors whole, and use the time to build the energy infrastructure required to actually turn the buildings on.
